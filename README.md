# Home Security Checklist for Northport and Tuscaloosa, Alabama

A practical, open checklist for homeowners, renters and landlords in Northport, Tuscaloosa and the surrounding parts of West Alabama. It covers doors, locks, keys, garages, windows and lockout planning, and it is written so anyone can work through it in a weekend.

Feel free to fork it, adapt it to your own home, or open an issue with suggestions.

---

## Why a Checklist?

Most homes are not broken into because of some sophisticated technique. They are broken into because a door was left unlocked, a cheap strike plate gave way to a kick, or an old key was still floating around from a previous owner or tenant. Small, boring fixes prevent most of these problems.

Northport and Tuscaloosa have a mix of older neighborhoods, new subdivisions, student rentals and rural properties. That variety means security needs differ from house to house, but the fundamentals stay the same.

---

## 1. Know Who Has Your Keys

- [ ] List every key to your home, including spares with family, neighbors, cleaners and dog walkers.
- [ ] If you bought or rented a home recently, assume previous owners, tenants, contractors or realtors may still have copies.
- [ ] Rekey all exterior locks after moving in. Rekeying changes the pins so old keys stop working while you keep the existing hardware.
- [ ] Rekey again any time a key is lost, stolen or not returned.

Rekeying is usually much cheaper than replacing locks, and it is the single most effective step after a move.

---

## 2. Check Every Exterior Door

For the front door, back door, side doors and the door between the garage and the house:

- [ ] Each door has a deadbolt, not just a knob lock.
- [ ] The deadbolt throws fully (about 1 inch) and does not rub or stick.
- [ ] The strike plate is secured with 3-inch screws that reach the wall stud.
- [ ] Hinges on outward-opening doors use non-removable pins or security screws.
- [ ] Solid-core or metal doors are used on exterior openings where possible.
- [ ] Glass near the lock is reinforced or the lock cannot be reached by breaking the glass.

A door that has to be slammed or lifted to lock is a door that will eventually be left unlocked. Fix alignment problems early.

---

## 3. Choose the Right Locks

- [ ] Use ANSI/BHMA Grade 1 or Grade 2 deadbolts on main exterior doors.
- [ ] Consider keying all exterior doors alike so one key opens them all.
- [ ] If you install a smart lock or keypad, start with a solid mechanical deadbolt underneath it.
- [ ] Change default codes and use unique codes for each person.
- [ ] Keep a mechanical backup for smart locks in case of dead batteries.

---

## 4. Sliding Doors and Windows

- [ ] Add a security bar or dowel in the track of sliding glass doors.
- [ ] Install anti-lift blocks or screws in the upper track so the panel cannot be lifted out.
- [ ] Make sure every window latch works and is actually used.
- [ ] Add window locks or pins on ground-floor windows.
- [ ] Trim shrubs that hide windows from the street.

---

## 5. Garage and Outbuildings

- [ ] Keep the garage door closed when you are not in the garage.
- [ ] Lock the door between the garage and the house as if it were a front door.
- [ ] Secure the emergency release on the garage door opener with a shield or zip tie so it cannot be fished from outside.
- [ ] Do not leave garage remotes in vehicles parked outside.
- [ ] Lock sheds and workshops with a quality padlock and hasp; tools stored there can be used to break into the house.

---

## 6. Lighting and Visibility

- [ ] Motion-activated lights cover all entry doors and the driveway.
- [ ] Porch lights work and are on a timer or sensor.
- [ ] House numbers are clearly visible from the street for emergency responders.
- [ ] Indoor lights or smart plugs run on a schedule when you travel.

---

## 7. Spare Keys and Lockout Planning

- [ ] Do not hide a key under the mat, in a planter or in a fake rock.
- [ ] Leave a spare with a trusted neighbor or family member instead.
- [ ] Keep a spare car key at home.
- [ ] Replace key fob and smart lock batteries before they die.
- [ ] Save the number of a local, licensed locksmith in your phone before you ever need it.

When choosing a locksmith in Alabama, ask for the company name and a clear total price before work begins, and expect to show ID so they can verify you live at the address. Alabama locksmiths are licensed through the Alabama Electronic Security Board of Licensure.

## 8. For Renters

- [ ] Ask the landlord whether locks were rekeyed after the previous tenant.
- [ ] Get permission in writing before changing or adding locks.
- [ ] Keep original hardware if you install your own lock, so it can be restored at move-out.
- [ ] Report broken locks, latches or windows in writing right away.

Student rentals near campus turn over every year, which makes rekeying between tenants especially important.

---

## 9. For Landlords and Property Managers

- [ ] Rekey every unit between tenants.
- [ ] Keep a key log showing who has which keys.
- [ ] Consider keypad locks for high-turnover units so codes can be changed without a service call.
- [ ] Use a master key system for multi-unit buildings.
- [ ] Inspect doors, strikes and windows at every turnover.

---

## 10. After a Break-In or Attempt

- [ ] Call 911 if anyone is in danger or the intruder may still be inside.
- [ ] Do not touch damaged areas until police have documented them.
- [ ] Photograph the damage for insurance.
- [ ] Secure the door the same day. A damaged frame or broken lock leaves the home exposed.
- [ ] Rekey or replace locks if keys were taken.
- [ ] Reinforce the strike plate and frame, since kicked doors usually fail there.

---

## 11. Seasonal Maintenance

Twice a year (a good reminder is when the clocks change):

- [ ] Test every lock and deadbolt.
- [ ] Lubricate lock cylinders with a graphite or dry lubricant, not oil.
- [ ] Tighten loose strike plates and hinges.
- [ ] Replace smart lock and fob batteries.
- [ ] Check outdoor lights and motion sensors.
- [ ] Review who has keys and codes.

Alabama humidity and summer heat can cause doors to swell and stick, so seasonal checks catch alignment problems before they become lockouts.

---

## Quick Priority List

If you only have one afternoon, do these first:

1. Rekey exterior locks if you do not know who has keys.
2. Install 3-inch screws in every deadbolt strike plate.
3. Secure sliding doors with a bar and anti-lift blocks.
4. Lock the garage-to-house door.
5. Remove hidden spare keys and give one to a trusted person.
6. Save a local locksmith's number.
